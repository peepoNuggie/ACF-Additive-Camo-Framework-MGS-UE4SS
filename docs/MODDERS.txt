================================================================================
  ACF - Additive Camo Framework  v1.1
  Making a camo mod that targets an ACF slot
================================================================================

ACF gives you five new camouflage slots. Your mod fills one of them, and the
player keeps every vanilla camo.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SHORT VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Ship a CamouflageAssetType asset named Camouf_<ID>_asset at

    /Game/Maps/AssetCamouflage/Camouf_<ID>_asset

where <ID> is 61 to 65. That is the whole contract. ACF does the rest: the slot
is unlocked automatically, named, and given an icon.

    slot         camo ID     shown in game as
    ---------------------------------------------
    1            61          ACF Mod 1
    2            62          ACF Mod 2
    3            63          ACF Mod 3
    4            64          ACF Mod 4
    5            65          ACF Mod 5

There are only five, and that is a hard limit of the game. Slots 1 to 4 are the
reserved uniform entries it already knows about (ADDITIONAL_UNIFORM_2 through 5).
Slot 5 is the DOWNLOAD placeholder, which the equip menu's list builder skipped
outright until ACF patched that out.

Nothing above 65 is reachable. 66 was an internal sentinel, 67 to 69 are
cardboard boxes the player already owns, and none of 66 to 69 appear in the
game's id-to-resource map at all, so they have nothing to attach to.

TWO DIFFERENCES ON SLOT 5.

1. ITS CONCEALMENT VALUES DO NOT WORK. BaseCamo and the per-terrain grid are read
   from your file correctly, and ignored by the game. Slot 5 conceals as Tiger
   Stripe does, whatever you write.

   Measured: with the grid set to 31/41/51 on brown soil the game returned Tiger
   Stripe's 30/60/75 instead, and BaseCamo contributed nothing at all. Slots 1 to
   4 are unaffected and honour both. The cause is not yet known.

   Everything else on slot 5 is unaffected, because the abilities read the uniform
   byte directly instead of going through that table - name, description, colors,
   thumbnail, INFAmmoFlag, INFAmmoWeapon, AnimalsSA, INFSuppressor and SilentSteps
   all behave exactly like slots 1 to 4.

   If concealment matters to your mod, use slots 1 to 4. Known issue, being worked
   on - see the Nexus page for current status.

2. Its name is limited to 15 characters. It borrows a menu row the game only ever
   labelled "UNLOCKED", and the buffer behind that word holds 15 and no more. A
   longer Name= is ignored rather than cut short, and the row shows "ACF Mod 5".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BUILDING THE ASSET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The easiest route is to use RRACF
RRACF - Companion Tool for ACF - https://www.nexusmods.com/metalgearsoliddeltasnakeeater/mods/236

The other route is to clone an existing camo asset and repoint it.

1. Extract a vanilla camo asset to edit as a template:

       retoc to-legacy <game>\Content\Paks <out> --version UE5_3 -f "Camouf_12_asset"

   Camouf_12 (Sneaking Suit) is a good template because it uses both materials
   and meshes. Camouf_5 (Splitter) is material-only and simpler.

2. Open it in UAssetGUI and repoint its Import entries at your own materials and
   meshes. You do not need to touch the export/property data - the properties
   reference imports by index, so changing what an import resolves to is enough.

3. Rename it to Camouf_61_asset (or 62/63/64/65). Renaming needs TWO edits:

       * the Name Map entries (short name and full package path)
       * General Information -> PackageName

   Miss the PackageName and the asset keeps the original's chunk ID, which means
   it silently OVERRIDES the camo you cloned instead of becoming a new one. This
   is the single most common mistake.

4. Package it:

       repak pack <folder>
       retoc to-zen <in.pak> <out.utoc> --version UE5_3

5. Ship the resulting .pak, .ucas and .utoc together, to be placed in
   Content\Paks\mods\. All three are required.

A camo that only changes textures does not need new meshes - clone the material
instances and repoint BaseColor_NonVT at your texture.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTIONAL: NAME, DESCRIBE AND TUNE YOUR SLOT                   [ACF v1.1 or newer]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

By default a slot is labeled "ACF Mod 1" through "ACF Mod 5" and conceals no
better than being Naked. To change that, ship a plain text file next to your pak:

    Content\Paks\mods\MyCamo_P.pak        <- your camo
    Content\Paks\mods\ACF_Slot61.txt      <- your metadata

The number in the filename is the slot you are targeting (61 to 65). Contents:

    Name=Ocelot's Uniform
    PlainDesc=Worn by the young Ocelot.
    BaseCamo=0

These appear in the Survival Viewer and in the Camouflage Collection under
Extras.


COLORED DESCRIPTION LINES

The description is written as up to four keys, one per color. ACF assembles
them, in this order, one per line:

    PlainDesc=Worn by the young Ocelot.
    AbilityDescOrange=Draws faster from the hip.
    WarningDesc=Offers no concealment in water.
    SpecialDesc=Never runs out of ammunition.

    PlainDesc            plain white
    AbilityDescOrange    orange   - what the uniform lets you do
    WarningDesc          red      - the drawback
    SpecialDesc          yellow   - a special effect

Leave any of them blank, or delete the line, and it is skipped entirely -
nothing is printed for it and no gap is left.

These are the game's own colors, matching how vanilla entries are written:
Spider is a plain line followed by an orange one. They are also the ONLY four.
There is no way to pick an arbitrary color, because the names come from a style
table inside the game that cannot have rows added to it.

The older single Description= key still works and is used only when none of the
four are present, so files written before this existed are unaffected. It takes
raw markup - <Ability>text</>, closing with </> rather than </Ability> - which is
exactly what ACF writes for you from the four keys above. Prefer the four keys.

BaseCamo is a real camouflage value, not a label. It is added to the camouflage
index the game calculates for you, so terrain, stance and grass still apply on
top - your slot simply conceals better or worse than bare skin by that amount
everywhere. Positive hides you, negative exposes you.

Sensible range is roughly -100 to +100. It is stored as a single signed byte, so
anything beyond -128 to 127 will not do what you expect.

BaseCamo is the SIMPLE option, and it is not how the game itself works. No
vanilla camo has a base value - every one of them is 0 in this field, and their
entire strength comes from the per-terrain values below. (Two things that look
like exceptions are not: Gold's -100 is applied by game code rather than this
field, and Black is the one camo carrying a flat value, 25.)

So use BaseCamo when you just want "this uniform hides a bit better than bare
skin" and do not want to think about it. For anything that should feel like a
real camo, leave BaseCamo=0 and fill in the grid instead.


PER-TERRAIN VALUES

This is how vanilla camos actually work, and where the interesting design is.
A camo is not one number - it has a separate value for each of the 27 surfaces
the game recognizes, and for each of five stances. That is why Water works
underwater, Snow works against white, and going prone in grass helps.

With BaseCamo left at 0, the numbers you write here ARE the percentages the game
shows, directly comparable to any vanilla camo. If you do set both, they add -
BaseCamo=30 with a grass value of 35 gives 65 in grass, not 35 - which is
usually not what you want. Pick one approach.

Add lines like this to the same file:

    ;                 stand crouch prone  wall  wallcr
    CamoGrass=         35,   50,   80,    55,    60
    CamoSoilBrown=     30,   60,   75,    65,    70
    CamoWhite=        -10,   20,   40,    30,    35

Those three are Tiger Stripe's real numbers, so they show the shape of a jungle
camo and roughly what stance is normally worth. Note the negative - a good camo
is bad somewhere, and deciding where yours struggles is most of the design.

A surface you leave out gets BaseCamo in every stance, so with BaseCamo=0 an
unlisted surface conceals like bare skin. You may also write a single number
(CamoGrass=35) and ACF uses it for all five, though then going prone gains you
nothing there, which is not how vanilla camos behave.

Surfaces are picked by color and material, not by area. Standing by a tree you
may be on SoilBrown, then ObjBrown pressed against the trunk, then Grass a step
later. The template lists all 27 names.

The game still applies its own environmental modifiers - light level, time of day
and so on - on top of the result, exactly as it does for a vanilla camo.

Run  acfslots  to see how many surfaces ACF read from each file, and to be told
about any line it could not parse. A line with a typo is silently treated as
absent, so check this after editing.


ABILITIES

These work today, and each is one key in the same file. The templates document them
fully; briefly:

    INFAmmoFlag=1        every weapon costs no ammo        (like Infinity Facepaint)
    INFAmmoWeapon=...    only the weapons you list         (like Grenade Camo)
    AnimalsSA=1          no shaking while aiming           (like Animals camo)
    INFSuppressor=1      your suppressor never wears out
    SilentSteps=1        your footsteps make no noise      (like the Sneaking Suit)

All of them mirror something a vanilla camouflage already does, so none is a new
ability - they just make it available to your slot.

SilentSteps is real silence rather than a volume reduction: the game has a single
check for "does this uniform silence footsteps", shared by the Sneaking Suit, the
White Tuxedo and Spirit, and your slot is added to it. Only the footsteps - Spirit's
CQC stamina drain and the Tuxedo's life recovery are separate and not included.

INFSuppressor only ever PREVENTS the count dropping. A suppressor has a use count,
not a percentage: it starts at 250 and loses one per shot. Picking up or repairing
one still raises it as normal, so this cannot refill a spent suppressor.


TEMPLATES

You do not have to write these files by hand. Ready-made templates for all five
slots are on the ACF Nexus page under MISCELLANEOUS FILES:

    "ACF Config Files for Modders"

Download it, drop the ACF_Slot<ID>.txt for the slot you are using next to your
pak, and edit it. Every surface is already listed at 0.

The templates also list keys that do NOT work yet - special effects, and movement
and health multipliers. They are written down so the file format does not have to
change when those arrive. ACF ignores them today, and leaving them in causes no
harm.

Notes:

  * Blank lines are fine. Lines starting with ; or # are comments.
  * Spaces around the = are fine.
  * If the file is missing, the slot keeps its default name. Nothing breaks.
  * Put the file inside your archive at Content\Paks\mods\ so mod managers place
    it correctly - Vortex and MO2 preserve the folder structure in your zip.

To check what ACF read, use the console command:

    acfslots

It prints all five slots whether or not a file was found, so a missing file is
easy to tell apart from one that failed to parse.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTIONAL: YOUR OWN THUMBNAIL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each slot draws its icon from a texture the base game already ships:

    slot 1 (61)   Plugins/CobraUI/Content/Textures/sv/camouflage/9200220
    slot 2 (62)   .../9265756
    slot 3 (63)   .../9331292
    slot 4 (64)   .../9396828
    slot 5 (65)   .../320302

Every slot has its OWN number even though ACF puts the same logo on all five.
That is deliberate: it means you can replace one slot's thumbnail without
touching any other.

Those are 256x128 DXT5, stored inline in the .uexp with this layout:

    113 bytes header | 32768 bytes DXT5 pixel data | 28 bytes trailer

So you can swap the art without any resizing: keep the header and trailer, and
drop in the 32768 bytes that follow the 128-byte header of a 256x128 DXT5 .dds.
Verified - the payload region of ACF's own branded texture is byte-identical to
its source .dds with the 128-byte header removed.

Keep the .uasset exactly as it is. It is identical between the vanilla texture
and ACF's branded one, so the image lives entirely in the .uexp and there is
nothing in it to edit.

Two things that do NOT work, both tried:

  * Taking one texture's .uexp and pairing it with a DIFFERENT texture's
    .uasset. They are the same size and the same format, and the container even
    passes `retoc verify` - but the game cannot load the result and draws a
    letter badge ("U.K") instead of art. Splice the pixels into the .uexp that
    belongs to the asset; do not swap whole files between assets.

  * Pointing a slot at a texture nothing else uses. The row stores a hard
    UObject pointer rather than a path, so the texture has to be one the game
    already loads. An unused texture is never loaded, so it can never resolve -
    "unused" and "usable" are mutually exclusive here.

ACF already overrides all five with its own logo. If you override the one for
your slot, yours wins if your pak loads later - but that is load-order dependent,
so mention it in your description rather than relying on it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHECK YOUR CHUNK ID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

IoStore finds assets by a hash of the package path, not by filename. If yours
does not match what the game expects, the pak mounts and does nothing at all -
no error, no warning.

For a NEW asset (Camouf_61_asset), the ID must be unique - compare it against the
asset you cloned and confirm they differ:

    retoc list --path <your>.utoc

For an OVERRIDE (a thumbnail), the ID must be IDENTICAL to vanilla's:

    retoc list --path <game>\Content\Paks\pakchunk0-Windows.utoc | findstr 9200220
    retoc list --path <yours>.utoc | findstr 9200220


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLOT COLLISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If two installed mods use the same slot, one silently disappears - last pak
loaded wins.

Please:

  * State clearly in your mod description which slot it uses.
  * Consider uploading alternate files for different slots so users can avoid
    clashes.
  * Put the slot number in the filename, e.g. MyCamo_ACF_Slot2.pak.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TESTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With ACF installed, load a save and open the Survival Viewer. Your camo should
appear in its slot and be selectable immediately - no console commands.

If the slot is listed but shows Olive Drab, the asset is not resolving. Check the
name is exactly Camouf_<ID>_asset, the path is /Game/Maps/AssetCamouflage/, and
the chunk ID is unique.

UE4SS.log lines tagged [ACF] show what the framework resolved.
